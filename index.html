Who this is for

Picture someone who lost his dog and hasn't gotten a new one yet. He's an introvert. On every walk he passes dogs he would love to play with, and he never knows if he can ask.

This site exists so that he knows. And so that every owner whose dog needs space can say so without saying a word.

The signals

Each set answers the two questions every dog lover has: is this dog friendly? and is it OK to come say hi and play? We use traffic-light colours because almost everyone already knows them.

Green: come say hi

Friendly? Yes. Approach and play? Yes.

This dog loves people and other dogs. Ask the owner, then enjoy.

Yellow: ask first, give space

Friendly? Usually. Approach and play? Not right now.

Nervous, in training, healing or working. Please don't approach without asking.

Red: keep your distance

Friendly? No. Approach and play? No.

Please give this dog and its person plenty of room. Thank you.

These are guides, not guarantees. Always ask the owner before you touch any dog.

What you get: a matching set

A collar

Dogs are already used to collars, so this one stays on and seen. Wide enough to spot from a distance.

A leg sleeve

A soft, wide sleeve around the front leg, in the same colour, so people can see the signal from the side and from behind.

We recommend both for every dog. Two matching pieces are easier to see and harder to miss.

Why a sleeve and not a band

We started out with little bands. Then we worried a dog might scratch one off and choke on it, and we're not willing to risk a single one of our little buddies. So the leg piece is a soft, wide sleeve instead, and it's on us humans to put it on well.

Every design has to do three things:

  • Stay on through walks, play and scratching.
  • Never be a choking hazard: soft, wide, no small hard parts.
  • Be easy for people to see: bright, and never too skinny.

Sleeve concepts we're testing

Every leg piece will be a sleeve. Here's where each idea stands.

Leading first design

Hook-and-loop (Velcro) sleeve

Wraps around and closes flat. Adjusts to any leg, easy on and off, no hard parts.

Considering

Pull-on stretch sleeve

Slide it up the leg a little, then ease it back down so it sits comfortably with the fur.

Considering

Magnetic closure

Very easy to use, but a dog might be able to scratch it off. Needs hard thinking before we'd use it.

Future

Zip sleeve

Secure, but a zip is a hard part, so it has to be fully covered.

Ruled out

Snap bracelet

Too firm for a dog's leg.

How this will work

  1. You ask for a set. Free, with free shipping, while the first $5,000 lasts.
  2. We confirm the size by email, then make and mail your matching collar and sleeve.
  3. Your dog wears the colour on every walk.
  4. People see it and learn it. Every set on the street teaches a few more people what the colours mean.
  5. You share it. Tell your dog friends, and send them here for their own free set.

Our plan to make it catch on

For this to work, society has to see these colours everywhere. Others have tried colour-coded collars and leashes before, and it never caught on. For humanity to get past this problem, one effort has to break through, and we intend for this to be it.

Our bet is the combination: this site, every set free for everyone, and a plan that covers what usually goes wrong. The goal is one month when it's in the news and everyone gets excited, because from then on every dog lover can see whether they can go play with a dog on the street.
