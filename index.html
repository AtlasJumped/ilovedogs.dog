Uniting dogs and humans

Can I play with your dog?

Our vision: a world where every dog lover knows, at a glance, whether they can play with a dog or not.

And a world where every owner of a dog that isn't friendly can tell everyone, from across the street, to keep their distance. Every set is 100% free: a collar and a leg sleeve for your dog, and a matching band for you.

Who this is for

Picture someone who lost his dog and hasn't gotten a new one yet. He's an introvert. On every walk he passes dogs he would love to play with, and he never knows if he can ask.

This site exists so that he knows. And so that every owner whose dog needs space can say so without saying a word.

The movement

This isn't about a brand. It's about a code everyone knows.

We don't need everyone to buy our collars, or to donate forever. We want to drop a bomb of positivity on how people and dogs meet: one simple colour code that every dog lover understands.

It takes two halves to work. Owners give the signal, and everyone around them has to know what it means. So the message matters as much as the collar.

  1. Unaware: today, most people have never heard of a colour code for dogs.
  2. Aware: people see the colours, and they know how to act.
  3. Adopted: owners think, "I just need a red collar from any brand, and people will give us space."

It won't be perfect. Some owners of friendly dogs will love a red collar, and some owners of unfriendly dogs will buy green. That's why it's a guide, and you still ask first. What we gain is bigger: everybody who loves dogs knows how to act and share their love properly.

The colour code

One standard, the same for every brand and every dog. When you share it, please share it in exactly these words, so the message stays the same everywhere it goes.

Green: come say hi

Friendly with people and with dogs.

This dog is approachable. Say hello to the owner, then enjoy.

Yellow: ask first

Maybe not good with dogs, but loves people.

Always ask before you come closer, and keep your own dog back until the owner says yes.

Red: keep away

People and other dogs, please keep away.

Give this dog and its person plenty of room, and keep your dog away too. Thank you.

These are guides, not guarantees. Always ask the owner before you touch any dog.

What you get: a matching set

A collar

Dogs are already used to collars, so this one stays on and seen. Wide enough to spot from a distance.

A leg sleeve

A soft, wide sleeve around the front leg, in the same colour, so people can see the signal from the side and from behind.

A band for you

A wristband for the owner in the same colour, so people can read the code from your side of the leash too.

We recommend the collar and the sleeve for every dog. Two matching pieces are easier to see and harder to miss, and your band matches them.

Why a sleeve and not a band

We started out with little bands. Then we worried a dog might scratch one off and choke on it, and we're not willing to risk a single one of our little buddies. So the leg piece is a soft, wide sleeve instead, and it's on us humans to put it on well.

Every design has to do three things:

  • Stay on through walks, play and scratching.
  • Never be a choking hazard: soft, wide, no small hard parts.
  • Be easy for people to see: bright, and never too skinny.

Reserve a free set for your city

We don't mail sets one at a time. If one dog wore a green collar and nobody else did, nobody would know what it means, and that owner would give up. So we wait until a whole city is ready, then everyone gets their set on the same day.

A city is ready when all three bars are full: enough dog owners have signed up (our goal is 1 in 5 dog owners), there's enough money to make every set, and a volunteer team is ready to hand them out. We're aiming for spring 2027, but the meters decide, not a date.

We're putting in the first $5,000 ourselves: $2,500 for Los Angeles and $2,500 for Washington, DC. Every donation after that goes to more sets, shipping and getting the word out, in the city the donor picks.

Loading the city meters...

Not in LA or DC? Reserve anyway and tell us your city. Every city with enough sign-ups gets its own meters.

Which colour sets?

How this will work

  1. You reserve a set for your city. It's free.
  2. Your city's meters fill up: dog owners, money and volunteers.
  3. We make every set for that city and pack each one sealed and labelled with its owner's name.
  4. We ship one big box to a public spot, like Santa Monica beach for Los Angeles, and you can follow it here.
  5. Drop day: everyone picks up their set together, and the whole city sees the colours at once.
  6. Every walk teaches someone. People see the colours, learn the code and pass it on.

Drop day

Each drop is a big public event. One box goes to the hand-out spot instead of thousands of separate packages, which keeps the cost per set low. Inside, every set is sealed and labelled so volunteers can find yours fast.

We'll post the place and time here, with a live tracker for the box, as soon as a city's meters are full.

Our plan to make it catch on

For this to work, society has to see these colours everywhere. Others have tried colour-coded collars and leashes before, and it never caught on. For humanity to get past this problem, one effort has to break through, and we intend for this to be it.

Our bet is the combination: this site, every set free for everyone, and a plan that covers what usually goes wrong. The goal is one month when it's in the news and everyone gets excited, because from then on every dog lover can see whether they can go play with a dog on the street.

That's why we launch city by city, all at once. Thousands of dogs in the same colours on the same day is news. One dog alone isn't.
